Codex gpt-5.6-terra R3-L6 — opened Göpferich 2009/2006c, Groeben 1982, and Schriver’s ERIC reprint; best find: reverbalisation yields line-level diagnostic evidence through a produced rewrite, not a clarity rating.

**[Göpferich 2009, JoSTrans 11](https://jostrans.soap2.ch/issue11/art_goepferich.pdf)**  
**What / status:** Karlsruhe is an argued expert framework, not a new reader study (§§2–5, pp. 32–48).  
**Six dimensions:** concision—only necessary content/signs (§5.1, pp. 40–42); correctness—accurate, consistent content and encoding (§5.2, pp. 42–43); motivation—attract/maintain attention (§5.3, pp. 43–44).  
Structure—appropriate macro/micro sequence (§5.4, pp. 44–46); simplicity—target-group-adequate lexicon/syntax (§5.5, pp. 46–48); perceptibility—layout, typography, non-verbal structure (§5.6, p. 48).  
**Produce, not rate:** §5 is an expert checklist; it gives no reader production task, so it cannot satisfy terse’s reader rule.  
**Cost / collision:** it supports the truth pass’s correctness concern, but its rating/checklist form is precisely an unmeasured mandatory pass terse rejects.  
It also says reader methods are more reliable but time-consuming (§2, p. 32): a useful caution, not a sample-backed effect estimate.

**[Göpferich 2006c, HERMES 37](https://tidsskrift.dk/her/article/download/25870/22779)**  
**What / status:** measured method demonstration, five target-group subjects; all were highly educated translation students/graduates/lecturers, explicitly non-representative (§§1–2.1, pp. 68–71; n=5).  
**Reader produces:** a complete target-audience reverbalisation/optimised paraphrase in Translog; passages judged already adequate may be copied (§2.2, p. 72).  
They concurrently verbalise while working; keystrokes, clicks and pauses are logged, and they later produce unanswered specialist questions (pp. 72–73).  
**Per-detail labels:** number each changed source passage; juxtapose original/change; assign one Karlsruhe dimension; label improvement, deterioration, or neither; retain TAP reasons, criticisms without edits, and unanswered questions (§2.4, pp. 73–74).  
Aggregate each criticised detail by how many of five readers encountered it (p. 74; Table 3, pp. 86–88). This is production evidence, not an opinion question.  
**Cost / terse collision:** slow transcription, comparison and adjudication; it must be a separate diagnostic run, not an extra field in the scored fresh-reader brief.  
**Model fit:** a model can produce a bounded paraphrase, trace, and unanswered-detail list without rating; human-to-model validity is unmeasured, and model “thinking aloud” is not trustworthy concurrent process data.

**Hamburg model — opened only through Groeben’s primary 1982 account, not Langer et al.’s original volume**  
**What / status:** secondary assertion about Langer, Schulz von Thun and Tausch; Groeben II.3.1, pp. 188–198, reports their procedure but the original book was not available.  
**Derivation:** 18 impression features from prior research/rhetoric were rated by trained experts across texts, correlated, then factor-analysed into four dimensions; subsequent text ratings used +2 to −2 (pp. 190–193).  
**Four:** linguistic simplicity; arrangement/order; concision–precision; additional stimulation (pp. 191–193).  
**Authors’ qualitative weights:** simplicity “most important”; order “considerable”; concision “less decisive” but underestimated; stimulation only supplementary, and helpful only with strong order (pp. 196–197).  
There are no numeric weights in the opened account; it says their all-dimensions-at-once design cannot estimate exact empirical weights (p. 196).  
**Produce, not rate:** the reader/expert produces a five-point dimension rating, so it fails terse’s non-opinion constraint and should not be used with a model reader.

**[Groeben 1982, *Leserpsychologie*](https://psycharchives.prod.zpid.org/bitstream/69c382e4-efe7-40c2-b084-9b4f947862e0)**  
**What / status:** reports Groeben’s 3×3×2 text experiment in 18 Münster upper-secondary classes; individual participant count is not reported (pp. 203–206).  
**Reader produces:** serial word guesses before seeing later text (subjective-information measure), a cloze completion 1–2 hours later, and selected topics for further information—not ratings (pp. 73–76, 204–205).  
**Finding:** content structure explained 86% of explained variance in comprehensibility and linguistic simplicity 3.5%; retention depended only on content structure (p. 205).  
The inverse-U was for a combined retention-plus-curiosity criterion; interest alone had no meaningful effect (pp. 205–206).  
So “maximum comprehensibility lowers retention” is too strong: the opened evidence supports a mixed structure/conflict objective, not a clean delayed-retention reversal.  
**Model fit / cost:** blind serial guessing is non-opinion model output but measures predictability, not local defects; a delayed-memory analogue is unvalidated and contaminated by model prior knowledge.

**[Schriver 1990 technical report, author reprint of the 1989 IEEE article](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED318009.pdf)**  
**What / status:** argued literature review, not a reliability experiment; it says “most researchers agree” concurrent measures are more reliable (p. 25; no sample size).  
**Concurrent protocol:** a target reader performs the document’s intended task while the evaluator records real-time behaviour; behavior protocols record actions without talk, whereas think-alouds require verbalising anything occurring during the task (pp. 17–20).  
**Reader produces:** task performance plus an action trace, or a contemporaneous verbal protocol; the latter locates and diagnoses the obstacle by what/how/why it interfered (pp. 19–21).  
Retrospective production—recall, paraphrase, summary or inference—happens after reading and often cannot identify the offending feature (pp. 22–25).  
**Model fit:** automatic file/open/search traces survive as non-opinion concurrent data; model rationales do not establish cognitive process, so do not call them think-aloud evidence.  
**Cost / collision:** this directly contradicts treating all extra reader output as forbidden: ban retrospective clarity ratings, but retain a separate observable trace instrument.

**Model-safe second instrument — argued, unvalidated:** after a scored failure, use a fresh diagnostic seat to produce (1) an automatic ordered `.md` action trace, (2) a bounded target-reader paraphrase of each decisive passage, and (3) required unanswered details. Compare the produced paraphrase to the cited text and answer key; label unsupported/misleading/missing detail mechanically where possible. Do not ask whether it was clear.

## contradictions

- Göpferich warns that even optimal legibility, readability and comprehensibility do not guarantee usability for instructions (2009, §6, p. 49). A correct verbal answer is not task success.
- Groeben weakens the proposed retention claim: the reported inverse-U is composite retention-plus-curiosity, not retention alone.
- Schriver supports terse’s objection to post-hoc clarity ratings, but contradicts a blanket ban on reader verbal output: concurrent task production is diagnostically valuable.
