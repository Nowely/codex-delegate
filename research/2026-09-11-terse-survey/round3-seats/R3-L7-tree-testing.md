**[Donna Spencer, “Card-Based Classification Evaluation” (2003)](https://boxesandarrows.com/card-based-classification-evaluation/)**
- What it is: the originating paper-card method; sections “Preparation,” “Running the evaluation,” and “Analysis.”
- Measures: no outcome rates; Spencer asserts 10 minutes from 20 users gives substantial feedback—**asserted**, not stability evidence.
- Protocol: give a realistic scenario, show top-level category cards, then only the chosen child card; permit at most two choices per level and record every choice.
- Success/directness: not formalized as rates; first versus second choices and clustering around the intended classification item are the diagnostic.
- Cost: 10–15-minute moderated sessions, 10–15 scenarios per participant, a scribe, and manual transcription.
- Collision with terse: it measures category paths before implementation; terse starts after prose and records files, not heading choices.

**[Dave O’Brien, “Tree Testing” (2009)](https://boxesandarrows.com/tree-testing/)**
- What it is: Treejack’s conversion of Spencer’s method; “Participating,” “Creating,” “Results,” and “Lessons learned.”
- Measures: a government case reports success rising 31% to 67%, but reports no N or task-level data—**measured case result, insufficient for inference**.
- Protocol: participant receives one realistic find-it task and a text-only top-level tree, expands one level at a time, then selects, backtracks, or skips.
- Definitions: success = percentage selecting a designated correct answer; directness = reaching it without wandering/backtracking.
- Practice: about 10 randomized tasks limits learning; “at least 30” participants is **asserted** guidance, not a demonstrated stability threshold.
- Collision: its 30-person recommendation is incompatible with terse’s present one-reader-per-question design.

**[Optimal Help Center: tasks, paths, and task results (updated 2024)](https://support.optimalworkshop.com/en/articles/3852036-create-tasks-and-set-answers)**
- What it is: vendor instrument documentation; [Task results](https://support.optimalworkshop.com/en/articles/2626846-how-to-interpret-the-task-results-tab-in-tree-testing), [Paths](https://support.optimalworkshop.com/en/articles/2626840-understand-the-paths-tab-in-tree-testing).
- Measures: nothing generalizable; its percentages are illustrative UI examples.
- Practice (**asserted**): predeclare one or more correct destinations; success is selecting one regardless of backtracking; directness means no movement back up the tree.
- Practice (**asserted**): retain direct/indirect success, failure, and skip plus each full route; the first wrong/down-then-up node is the repair target.
- Crucial rule: a parent cannot be correct; make the parent’s own content an explicit leaf child, or stop the tested path there (“Why you can’t select parent nodes”).
- Sample: Optimal’s public FAQ says 20–30 exploratory, 50 unmoderated “reliable,” and 50 per comparison group—**asserted**, with no opened validation study.
- Collision: a Markdown H2 with H3 children cannot itself be a target without a synthetic `Overview` leaf.

**[NN/g, “Tree Testing: Evaluate Menu Labels and Categories” (2023, modified 2026)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tree-testing/)**
- What it is: method guidance; sections “What Is,” “Tasks,” “Qualitative vs Quantitative,” “Benefits,” and “Limitations.”
- Measures: nothing; this is **argued** guidance.
- Protocol: give only a hierarchical category tree and task; no layout, visual design, or content is required; define the correct leaf before launch.
- Directness: whether users bounce among categories or go directly to their selection; NN/g treats it as context for label clarity and a proxy for path confidence.
- Practice: scenario wording must not repeat navigation labels; use a small moderated pilot before a larger quantitative run.
- Collision: tree testing deliberately excludes prose, cross-links, search, and visual cues, so it cannot establish terse’s “true and in the right place” condition.

**[NN/g, “Card Sorting: Uncover Users’ Mental Models” (2024)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/card-sorting-definition/)**
- What it is: the inverse/generative method; sections “Definition,” “Prepare Materials,” “Conduct,” and “Limitations.”
- Measures: nothing; recommendations are **argued** guidance.
- Protocol: give each participant atomic content cards, ask them to group and then label them; open sorts supply no categories, closed sorts validate supplied ones.
- Practice: use open sorting to derive candidate groups and labels, then tree-test the resulting hierarchy; NN/g explicitly says a sort does not yield the final exact tree.
- Sample: at least 15 qualitative participants, 30–50 quantitative participants, and 30–50 cards are recommendations, not demonstrated thresholds.
- Collision: card sorting cannot reveal a complete navigation route; treating its clusters as a finished Markdown hierarchy would overclaim.

**[GOV.UK content-navigation reports (2014, 2018)](https://insidegovuk.blog.gov.uk/2018/11/21/making-content-supergroups-more-super/)**
- What it is: application to published guidance/document categories, not software-reference docs.
- Measures: GOV.UK tree-tested content-type headings with “thousands” of survey responses, repeated the same tasks after revision, and reports task-specific improvements; exact N and rates are absent—**measured but incompletely reported**.
- The team paired quantitative tree paths with lab research because no-content trees cannot show the normal examples users see.
- A separate manuals report says four publishing/content manuals were informed by card sorting, interviews, and workshops, but reports no participant count: [“Content guidance now in one place”](https://insidegovuk.blog.gov.uk/2014/10/07/content-guidance-now-in-one-place/).
- Cost: valid documentation use needs a second, contextual instrument; tree results alone did not explain all label failures.
- Collision: this supports a route test, but not treating success/directness as a complete documentation-quality score.

**[Pirolli & Card, “Information Foraging,” *Psychological Review* 106(4), pp. 643–675 (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.106.4.643)**
- What it is: the requested theoretical paper; bibliographic locus verified in [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1037%2F0033-295X.106.4.643).
- Evidence: **unopened primary**—the APA copy was access-blocked and [Unpaywall records no OA/repository copy](https://api.unpaywall.org/v2/10.1037/0033-295X.106.4.643?email=research%40example.org).
- Therefore I cannot honestly report that paper’s exact model wording, experiments, sample sizes, or measured predictions.
- It should not be cited as evidence that a wrong tree branch is caused by scent rather than by task wording or prior knowledge.

**[Pirolli, *Information Foraging Theory*, author-hosted Chapter 1 proof (2007)](https://www.peterpirolli.com/ewExternalFiles/31354_C01_UNCORRECTED_PROOF.pdf)**
- What it is: later author formulation, not a substitute for the unopened 1999 article; printed pp. 13–14 and 24.
- Model (**argued/formal**): people choose information-seeking strategies that maximize expected knowledge value relative to interaction cost; patches have differing payoff and access cost.
- The brief operational rule is: “maximize the value of external knowledge gained relative to the cost of interaction” (p. 13).
- Its ACT-Scent architecture computes action utility from the relation between interface content cues and user goals (p. 24): low directness is therefore consistent with weak cue–goal fit.
- Measures: Chapter 1 reports no empirical scent prediction or sample; do not promote this explanatory model into a causal diagnosis.
- Collision: terse can record branch paths, but needs a separate experiment before attributing those paths to “information scent.”

**Protocol against terse’s `.md` corpus — proposed synthesis, not measured**

- Build a temporary text-only tree rooted at the entry file: each reachable Markdown document is labelled by its H1, then its H2–H6 outline; retain nesting only, no paragraphs, code, examples, search, or visual styling.
- If a file is reached through multiple navigation routes, clone its H1 subtree under each route; a Markdown file with no entry-visible route is an **orphan**, not an arbitrary `Other` branch.
- Map each code-derived reader decision to a planned correct **leaf** before recruiting. For a parent section’s own prose, add a leaf such as `Overview`; otherwise test only naturally leaf headings.
- If no defensible navigation tree exists, first run an open card sort with one decision/topic per card; turn group patterns into a candidate tree, then validate it with tree testing.
- Give each fresh participant one scenario phrased in reader language, e.g. “Before trying this tool, where would you expect to learn what it may modify?”—never echo `Permissions` or a filename.
- Record selected leaf, complete click path, first wrong branch, every backtrack, time, skip, and whether selection matched a predeclared target.
- Score success separately from directness: success is a correct leaf even after recovery; direct success is a correct leaf reached without backtracking.
- Use 50 participants per homogeneous reader group only as an unvalidated vendor recommendation; 20–30 is directional. Human-method sample guidance does not validate 20–50 model calls as human substitutes.

## Contradictions

- terse’s current “steps” metric is files opened before answering, not a heading route or backtracking trace ([audit Step 5](/Users/ruliny/Git/agent-skills/plugins/terse/skills/audit/SKILL.md:80), [measure brief](/Users/ruliny/Git/agent-skills/plugins/terse/skills/audit/references/measure.md:50)). It cannot distinguish direct success from recovery after a wrong heading branch.
- The prior-art file already claims tree testing catches that gap ([prior art](/Users/ruliny/Git/agent-skills/plugins/terse/references/prior-art.md:187)); the implemented audit does not yet collect the required path data.
- A tree failure is not automatically terse’s current `findability` cause: no prose is present, so the test cannot know whether the fact is true or whether its body placement is right. It diagnoses an upstream taxonomy/label/path failure.
- Code can establish the factual answer key; it cannot establish the correct navigation destination. Keep a separate location key.
- Spencer’s 20, O’Brien’s 30, and Optimal’s 50 are incompatible recommendations without an opened stability study. None supports calling terse’s one-trial, six-reader run stable.
