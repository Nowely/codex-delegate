**[Degani & Wiener, NASA CR-177549](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19910017830/downloads/19910017830.pdf)** — contractor report, May 1990; full PDF opened. Evidence labels below: ACC accident, INC incident narrative, FIELD observation/interview, EXP experiment, OP authors’ argument.
- What it measures: FIELD n=42 crews, 72 legs, ≈140 hours at one US carrier; interviews n=15 pilots from seven carriers; an unpublished tabulation of 20 airlines; simulator sample unreported. The 12,000-report ICAO collection, NTSB records and ASRS narratives were deliberately not statistically analysed, and ASRS examples are declared nonrepresentative (§1.3, printed pp5–6/PDF 8–9).
- Execution/device rules: explicitly choose call-do-response versus independent challenge-verification-response and assign roles/cross-checks (§§2.1, 3.1.4, pp7,18–19 [FIELD+OP]); keep the procedure visibly in use, process one item then its response, and end with a written completion call (§§5.2–5.3, pp29–31 [FIELD+INC+OP]); visibly distinguish current/completed/deferred items, preserve skipped items, and give state colours/labels one consistent meaning (§2.2, pp8–17 [OP; two reported EXPs, n absent; INC n=1]).
- Content/language rules: separate terse execution lists from expanded training material (§2.1.2, p8 [OP]); include critical cues rather than every action or a remedy for discipline, tune length to operating frequency, split long lists into chunks, and control revisions (§§3.1.5–3.2.3, 7.1.1 [FIELD+OP]); standardize vocabulary/names, but never impose another system’s sequence merely for consistency (§§3.3, 6.2.1–.4 [survey n=20+FIELD+OP]).
- Verification rule, verified: a response must state the observed status or value—not “checked”, “set” or “completed”; point at/touch the actual object (§§6.2.3, 7.2.2, pp41,49–50/PDF 44,52–53 [INC n=1+OP]). Delta 1141 also produced nominally correct flap callouts in under one second while the flaps/slats remained unset (§6.1.1, pp38–39 [ACC]).
- Interruption rules: use dependable initiation cues but anticipate cues disappearing; do not execute from memory or batch several items under one acknowledgement; provide checkoff/progress, an explicit hold/resume method, and restart a short section when place is uncertain; rerun the full procedure after outside work invalidates abbreviated-procedure assumptions (§§5.1–5.4, pp29–37 [FIELD+INC+OP]). The quoted 1/100 versus 3/1000 omission probabilities are Swain–Guttman estimates, not measurements in this study (§5.4.4, p36).
- Ordering rules, verified: prerequisites first (§7.2.1 [OP]); for otherwise order-flexible items, follow the reader’s physical/eye flow (§7.2.2 [reported EXP, n absent+OP]); align with external workflow only when this will not defer an unavailable item (§7.2.3 [INC n=1+OP]); critical items early override both flow rules, transient critical state is checked early and rechecked before use, and recovery buffers outrank adjacency (§§7.2.4–.5, 8.2.2; Appendix A rules 8–13, pp49–57,66–67 [Delta/Northwest ACC+INC+OP]). Respect operating culture and acquired-system knowledge, but not at the expense of “killer” items; design against production pressure (Appendix A 14–16 [Herald ACC+FIELD+INC+OP]).
- Cost/collision: task analysis, explicit state/roles, interruption machinery and duplicated critical checks consume authoring and execution time. The authors warn that their 16 guidelines are proposals, not specifications, and can conflict (Appendix A, p66). §8.2.2 contradicts a singular “move to the decision” remedy when that means semantic or last-action adjacency.

**[DOE-STD-1029-92, Change Notice 1](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/DOE-STD-1029-92_Chg_Notice_1.pdf)** — full 123-page official copy opened; the hosting page marks it archived/cancelled, and the document calls itself a model guide whose guidance is applied “as appropriate”, not requirements (§1.2, p2/PDF 20).
- What it measures: nothing. It reports no trial, accident series or sample. §4.10.1 p48 says an unidentified “industry study” attributed one quarter of human-performance events to deficient warnings, but supplies no sample or citation there; all practices below are therefore **asserted/opinion**, not measured.
- Derivation/test rules: collect governing sources, operating history and user interviews, then observe or simulate the work in its real setting (§2.3, pp5–6); choose detail so sequence, equipment location and decisions are executable without unspecified help (§3.1.1, pp10–11); use consistent, concrete terms, exact identifiers, instrument-matched precision and directly usable ranges (§3.1.2–.5, pp11–14).
- Sentence/logic rules: imperative verb first with a precise object; put IF/WHEN before THEN; avoid vague qualifiers, mixed AND/OR logic and mental unit conversion (§§3.1.3, 4.1–4.2, pp11–12,37–42). Contrary to a blanket one-instruction rule, §4.1[8] explicitly says to consider combining multiple verbs that share one object; actions assigned to different people remain separate (pp38–40).
- Placement rules: prerequisites go before manipulation, except a performance check valid only immediately before use belongs in the procedure body (§3.5.3[13], p29); local warnings go immediately before and on the same page as their action, while global hazards are also repeated locally (§§3.4, 4.10.1, pp23–26,48–50); acceptance criteria and the failure response sit at the verifying step (§4.12, pp52–54).
- Sequence/state rules: write actions in execution order; if actions are genuinely unordered, arrange them by equipment/layout and explicitly permit any order (§§3.6, 4.3, pp33,42); encode alternatives, deadlines, concurrency, continuous/repeated actions, actual recorded values and consequence-scaled independent verification (§§4.4–4.9, pp43–48); references must name destination and return without bypassing prerequisites or warnings (§4.11, pp51–52).
- Completion/cost/collision: restore affected state, tools, permits and consumables (§3.7, pp33–34); use adjacent signoffs, hold points and explicit N/A rather than silently skipping (§4.13, pp54–57). Walkdowns, page-local repetition and validation cost substantially more than a prose pass, but support testable procedure behaviour. DOE supports proximity for warnings and short-validity checks—not moving every fact or critical setup action to the last moment.

**[ASD-STE100 official FAQ, archived 2025-03-12](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312063121id_/https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_faq.html)** — issuer-authored public FAQ opened with its [Issue 9 download/request page](https://web.archive.org/web/20250824215223id_/https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html) and [overview](https://web.archive.org/web/20250114163341id_/https://www.asd-ste100.org/about_STE.html); Issue 9 is January 2025 and is supplied free after a form is emailed (download-page lines 87–94).
- What it measures: nothing; no experiment, accident set or sample is reported. Claims that STE improves comprehension, translation and human-factor risk are **asserted** (overview lines 139–143; FAQ lines 533,547,561), not measured.
- Publicly documented package: writing rules are Part 1 and a controlled dictionary is Part 2; approved words generally have one meaning and one part of speech, one synonym is selected, and project-specific technical names/verbs remain available (FAQ lines 118–124,354,385). Thus STE is publicly documented as a bundle, not a word-count intervention.
- Publicly documented procedure practices: short sentences, one **topic** per sentence, mostly imperative/active instructions, descriptive passive only when necessary, and condition-first when the operator must know the condition before acting (FAQ lines 181,305–312,319–326). All are **asserted/opinion**.
- Not publicly verified: the opened pages do not disclose 53 rules/nine sections, approximately 900 approved/1,200 avoided words, the 20/25-word limits, or a one-instruction-per-sentence rule. “One topic” is not evidence for “one instruction”. I therefore cannot verify the exact three-way cap+dictionary+instruction bundle without Issue 9 itself.
- Cost/collision: correct STE writing requires technical expertise, strong English, knowledge of both rules and dictionary, and recommended C1 proficiency (FAQ lines 453–462); readers allegedly need no training (lines 598–605). The FAQ disclaims any guarantee of reading-grade compliance (lines 433–440). This proves terse’s cap-only comparison is not a test of STE, but supplies no measured reason to reinstate hard caps or a mandatory standards pass.

contradictions

- First correct the premise: NASA §8.2.2 does **not** report that late flap/trim placement was deliberate. It says it was “probably done” to check nearer takeoff (p54); that is the authors’ inference. The NTSB-grounded fact is that Delta 1141 began engine No. 3, received runway clearance within 15 seconds, and then expedited the remaining checklist while the flaps/slats were unset (§8.2.2, pp54–55 [ACC]).
- Terse is right when a stable fact, condition or warning informs one decision and can be consumed there without removing recovery time: place it immediately before that decision. DOE’s step-local warning and short-validity performance-check rules are examples.
- Physical-flow ordering is right only after causal dependencies and safety timing are satisfied: use it for otherwise independent actions so the reader searches less and can recover their place (§§7.2.1–.2).
- NASA is right when an action/check is critical and failure needs time to detect or recover: execute it at the earliest low-workload point at which it is valid. If later information can invalidate it, retain that early action and add a last-responsible-moment recheck (§7.2.5; Appendix A 10–12). The rule is “closest **safe** point”, not closest topical or physical point.
- Priority is therefore: hard dependencies → validity window → criticality/recovery buffer → physical flow/topical adjacency. Two independently entered or temporally distinct decisions can require two copies.
- Replace [audit’s singular remedy](/Users/ruliny/Git/agent-skills/plugins/terse/skills/audit/SKILL.md:100) with: “Place it at the earliest safe decision where it is valid; repeat it at an independently read or last-responsible decision when state can change.” [revise already contains the missing exception](/Users/ruliny/Git/agent-skills/plugins/terse/skills/revise/references/writing-rules.md:21): repetition at an independently read decision point is not redundancy.
