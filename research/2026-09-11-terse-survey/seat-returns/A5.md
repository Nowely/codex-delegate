## threats to validity

Unreported design details below are unresolved risks, not assertions that misconduct occurred.

1. **One trial per question:** Sampling variation is unestimated. Even treating question pairs as independent, three improvements and zero reversals give exact two-sided McNemar **p = .25**; this small experiment cannot distinguish improvement from noise reliably.
2. **Dependent units:** Twelve responses are six paired questions about one document, not twelve independent replications. Questions may share passages and facts.
3. **Questions authored by the repairer:** The author can choose deficiencies their intervention addresses and then optimize directly against those questions. No hidden questions test transfer.
4. **Key author also judging:** Shared interpretations and errors can make both the repair and its grade agree with a mistaken oracle. Freezing that oracle does not independently validate it.
5. **Controls selected after the fact:** Selection after baseline can identify legitimate preservation cases but also select lucky successes. Selection after follow-up makes preservation circular. The supplied record does not establish selection timing.
6. **Only two controls:** Two preserved items, each observed once after repair, establish only those observed outcomes; they cannot certify preservation across the document.
7. **No no-document arm:** Prior knowledge, guessing, or training exposure could supply answers attributed to documentation. Such an arm estimates documentation’s contribution; its absence alone would not invalidate a properly randomized old-versus-new comparison.
8. **No concurrent unchanged-document arm:** Repeating the original README under the same conditions would help separate repair effects from run variation and temporal changes.
9. **Bundled intervention:** Four passes, their order, added attention, and factual corrections changed together. No contribution can be assigned to any pass.
10. **No simpler or effort-matched comparator:** Correcting the false claims alone, repairing only measured failures, or spending equivalent effort on one revision might achieve the same result.
11. **Single repository:** Domain, complexity, maturity, audience, and project-specific conventions are unreplicated.
12. **Single document:** A README cannot establish performance on tutorials, API references, migration guides, or navigation across a documentation collection.
13. **Model readers as human proxies:** Fresh context does not establish representative expertise, language ability, goals, attention, or behavior. Shared model priors remain.
14. **Artificial access conditions:** Markdown-only access and a fixed entry point may exclude legitimate reader resources or impose navigation behavior unlike actual use.
15. **Instrumentation and protocol drift:** Model/version, effort, temperature, tools, prompts, budgets, and enforcement need to remain comparable. Self-reported paths or departures require verification.
16. **Scoring validity:** Binary correctness hides partial understanding, unsupported correct guesses, consequential mistakes, and ambiguous acceptable answers. Independent grading reliability is unreported.
17. **Narrow outcome coverage:** Equal question weighting can obscure severity. Answering a quiz does not establish successful execution, retention, transfer, speed, or preservation of untested warnings.
18. **Departure interpretation:** A reader staying inside documentation may be better served—or merely more willing to guess. One fewer departure cannot identify the mechanism.
19. **Selection and stopping:** Repository selection, discarded drafts, retries, alternative questions, and stopping after a perfect score are unreported; each could inflate the apparent gain.
20. **Ceiling and reproducibility:** 6/6 leaves no measured headroom and does not imply universal correctness. Missing raw responses, scoring decisions, version identifiers, and the failure-to-question mapping limit independent reconstruction.

## cheapest decisive experiment

Test **incremental selection value**: before drafting, designate the repair-first writer as the single-writer baseline. Produce the prescribed three candidates and let two blinded judges select their winner using the known failures. Independently prepare ten hidden decision questions, including preservation cases, that neither writers nor selection judges see.

Evaluate baseline and winner on those questions with three fresh readers per question per version, interleaving versions and grading against an independently verified key while blind to candidate identity. Analyze the ten question-level differences; do not count repeated readers as independent documents. A reproducible hidden-question advantage without factual or preservation regressions would support a local benefit. A baseline advantage would count against it; an uncertain tie remains inconclusive.

**Cost:** Three writer calls, two selection-judge calls, and **60 reader calls**, plus independent question/key preparation and blind grading. Relative to evaluating only the winner, the comparison adds **30 reader calls**. At an assumed 5,000 input and 300 output tokens per reader call, total reading costs 300,000 input plus 18,000 output tokens; dollars equal **0.30 × input price + 0.018 × output price**, using per-million-token prices. Writer/judge charges and human time cannot be determined from the supplied evidence.

This is decisive only about a sufficiently clear local comparison. General superiority, cost-effectiveness, and the optimal number of writers or judges still require replication.
