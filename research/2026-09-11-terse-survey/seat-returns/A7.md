**T verdict — a distinct page classifier and reorganization planner.** It scores five types, adding way-finding; reports primary type, mixed status, confidence and reasons; uniquely supplies explicit abstention: **“unclassifiable. Do not force a classification.”** Mixed pages receive proposed output titles/types and optional cuts; MkDocs receives specific navigation instructions ([SKILL.md:38–95](https://github.com/trogera/diataxisSkills/blob/8370801/.skills/docs-reorg/SKILL.md#L38-L95)).
Its distinctive scoring rule is **“two or more” signals** before a type registers. However, the same table says one or two secondary signals are insufficient for “mixed,” while another rule treats any registered secondary type as mixed. Low confidence also means mixed here but triggers abstention in the main file. **Judgment: useful inspectable heuristic, internally ambiguous rather than calibrated scoring** ([references/page-types.md:22–36](https://github.com/trogera/diataxisSkills/blob/8370801/.skills/docs-reorg/references/page-types.md#L22-L36); [SKILL.md:50–54](https://github.com/trogera/diataxisSkills/blob/8370801/.skills/docs-reorg/SKILL.md#L50-L54)).
A useful unique exception is **“Mood within steps is not a mixed signal”**: declarative step results remain with instructions; conceptual prose outside steps receives different treatment ([references/page-types.md:49–59](https://github.com/trogera/diataxisSkills/blob/8370801/.skills/docs-reorg/references/page-types.md#L49-L59)).

**K verdict — a distinct governance/audit rubric.** It applies the two axes and reports confidence plus evidence. High means both axes clear; medium means one clear; low means conflicting/weak signals. Despite the main instruction to choose exactly one quadrant, its reference explicitly says **“Mixed content is flagged rather than forced into a single classification.”** There is no explicit no-fit/abstention category in that procedure ([SKILL.md:33–45,114–123](https://github.com/keithpatton/diataxis-agent-skill/blob/5b095a5/diataxis/SKILL.md#L114-L123); [references/compass.md:158–186](https://github.com/keithpatton/diataxis-agent-skill/blob/5b095a5/diataxis/references/compass.md#L158-L186)).
Its distinctive audit outputs include **“Identify gaps (missing quadrants)”** and **“reference-heavy, tutorial-poor”**; generation validates quadrant constraints, while restructuring proposes splits for confirmation. **Judgment: explicit category-coverage audit, but category absence is only a proxy for unanswered reader questions** ([SKILL.md:90–110](https://github.com/keithpatton/diataxis-agent-skill/blob/5b095a5/diataxis/SKILL.md#L90-L110)).

**P verdict — a distinct evidence-backed documentation production workflow.** The compass runs at sentence/paragraph and document scales; survey records section-level quadrants or “misclassified”/“blurred.” These classification instructions specify neither confidence scores nor a no-fit category ([references/compass.md:29–38](https://github.com/PeterKnego/diataxis-docs-skill/blob/de11e3b/skills/diataxis-docs/references/compass.md#L29-L38); [SKILL.md:64–68](https://github.com/PeterKnego/diataxis-docs-skill/blob/de11e3b/skills/diataxis-docs/SKILL.md#L64-L68)).
Its distinctive plan records **“the user need it serves”** and **“the source material backing it”**, names destinations for every move/split, and marks unsupported quadrants **“not created”** with a reason and remedy. That is an insufficient-evidence response, distinct from classifying a page as no-fit ([SKILL.md:75–97](https://github.com/PeterKnego/diataxis-docs-skill/blob/de11e3b/skills/diataxis-docs/SKILL.md#L75-L97)).
After classification it writes quadrants, checks sections, parks displaced material, and wires navigation; **“Misplaced content is always moved, never deleted”** differs materially from T’s **“Content to cut entirely”** ([P SKILL.md:157–185,221](https://github.com/PeterKnego/diataxis-docs-skill/blob/de11e3b/skills/diataxis-docs/SKILL.md#L157-L221); [T SKILL.md:73–78](https://github.com/trogera/diataxisSkills/blob/8370801/.skills/docs-reorg/SKILL.md#L73-L78)).
P additionally requires source-file/line evidence for reference claims and execution verification of tutorials, with explicit unverified fallbacks—concrete mechanisms beyond classification ([references/reference.md:26–31](https://github.com/PeterKnego/diataxis-docs-skill/blob/de11e3b/skills/diataxis-docs/references/reference.md#L26-L31); [references/tutorials.md:40–57](https://github.com/PeterKnego/diataxis-docs-skill/blob/de11e3b/skills/diataxis-docs/references/tutorials.md#L40-L57)).

**7. ARES and DeepEval**

**ARES measures** retrieved-context relevance, answer faithfulness to context, and answer relevance to the question ([docs/ares-doc/docs/faq.md:35–40](https://github.com/stanford-futuredata/ARES/blob/c7c9018/docs/ares-doc/docs/faq.md#L35-L40)).
**Human validation:** README requests ≥50 labeled triples, ideally hundreds; code reports judge/label agreement. PPI subtracts mean judge-minus-human error from the unlabeled prediction mean and constructs an asymptotic confidence interval for the aggregate. The optional machine-label route is a different evidentiary basis ([README.md:82–86](https://github.com/stanford-futuredata/ARES/blob/c7c9018/README.md#L82-L86); [ares/RAG_Automatic_Evaluation/LLMJudge_RAG_Compared_Scoring.py:1159–1166,1406–1450](https://github.com/stanford-futuredata/ARES/blob/c7c9018/ares/RAG_Automatic_Evaluation/LLMJudge_RAG_Compared_Scoring.py#L1406-L1450); [ppi.py:77–86](https://github.com/stanford-futuredata/ARES/blob/c7c9018/ares/RAG_Automatic_Evaluation/ppi.py#L77-L86)).
**DeepEval measures** retrieval precision/recall/relevance, answer relevance/faithfulness, and custom criteria such as correctness against an expected answer ([docs/content/guides/guides-rag-evaluation.mdx:73–79,166–175](https://github.com/confident-ai/deepeval/blob/f94d940/docs/content/guides/guides-rag-evaluation.mdx#L73-L79); [guides-answer-correctness-metric.mdx:8–18](https://github.com/confident-ai/deepeval/blob/f94d940/docs/content/guides/guides-answer-correctness-metric.mdx#L8-L18)).
**Human validation:** its guide prescribes expert pass/fail labels, TP/TN/FP/FN comparison, then tuning criteria, steps or thresholds. That section supplies neither a held-out protocol nor an agreement result; I could not establish a framework-wide human-validated accuracy from it. G-Eval research results are cited separately, not a validation of every custom judge ([docs/content/blog/llm-as-a-judge.mdx:411–431](https://github.com/confident-ai/deepeval/blob/f94d940/docs/content/blog/llm-as-a-judge.mdx#L411-L431); [docs/content/docs/(custom)/metrics-llm-evals.mdx:383–406](https://github.com/confident-ai/deepeval/blob/f94d940/docs/content/docs/%28custom%29/metrics-llm-evals.mdx#L383-L406)).
